Thirteen to seventeen. That was the age band, and choosing it narrow is the only reason the essay had anything specific to say.

The thesis was that teens in Canada and the United States in that range make decisions on the influence of the creators they follow rather than on the information available to them, and that the platform's structure rewards exactly that. I built the argument out of scholarly work on parasocial relationships and adolescent decision making, rather than out of the screen time commentary that was everywhere at the time.

The mechanism held up. Parasocial trust is not a bug in how teens watch, it is the product working as designed. The certainty did not hold up. I used the word indoctrinates in my own research question, and a stronger version of that essay would not have.

It is the earliest long piece of writing I would still hand to someone.
